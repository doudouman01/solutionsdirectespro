# Book Launch Checklist for Self-Published Authors: 40 Steps to a Successful Launch

Launching a book without a checklist is like flying a plane without a pre-flight inspection. Everything might be fine. Or you might forget one critical step that brings the whole thing down. The problem with book launches is that they involve dozens of interconnected tasks across multiple platforms, multiple timelines, and multiple audiences — and the consequences of forgetting something (a missing ARC reminder, an unoptimized description, a broken Amazon link) are often invisible until weeks later, when you notice the sales you never had.

This checklist exists to prevent that. Forty steps, organized by phase, covering every task from six weeks before launch through two weeks after. Use it for every book you publish. The tasks do not change — only the book does.

## Phase 1: Product readiness (6-4 weeks before launch)

### Is your manuscript truly done?

Before any launch planning begins, your manuscript must be final. Not almost final. Final. Every round of editing complete, every beta reader note addressed, every proofread correction applied. The version you launch is the version readers will review, and reviews do not update when you upload a revised manuscript.

The readiness checklist: developmental edit complete and changes implemented. Copy edit or line edit complete. Proofread complete — by someone other than you. Beta reader feedback received and addressed. Final manuscript formatted and exported to EPUB, MOBI (if needed), and print-ready PDF.

### Is your cover final?

Your cover must be complete and sized for all formats before you begin marketing. You need: the ebook cover (standard ratio, usually 2,560 x 1,600 pixels at minimum), the paperback full-wrap cover (front, spine, and back, sized to your exact page count and trim size using KDP's cover template), and social media versions (square crop for Instagram, vertical for BookTok, banner for Facebook).

The cover readiness checklist: ebook cover finalized and approved. Paperback wrap cover finalized (if offering print). Social media versions created. Thumbnail test passed — the cover is readable and genre-appropriate at 150 pixels wide, which is the size it displays in Amazon search results.

### Is your description ready?

Your book description must be written, HTML-formatted, and tested before you begin promoting. This means: hook written and tested against competitor descriptions. Full description structured with appropriate line breaks, bold text, and bullet points. HTML code tested in KDP's preview or Author Central. Description reviewed by at least one person who reads in your genre for emotional impact and clarity.

### Are your keywords and categories researched?

Seven keyword phrases selected, each targeting a different search query your ideal reader might use. Three initial browse categories chosen — the most specific available. List of additional categories to request through KDP support after publication prepared.

## Phase 2: Pre-launch marketing (4-2 weeks before launch)

### Is your preorder live?

Set up your book as a preorder on KDP. Upload the final manuscript (or a placeholder if final formatting is not complete — you can update it until 72 hours before the release date). Set the publication date. Verify that the listing looks correct: cover, description, pricing, and categories.

The preorder checklist: KDP preorder created. Publication date set. Pricing confirmed ($2.99-9.99 for 70% royalty tier). Cover and description verified on the live preorder page. Preorder link saved for use in all marketing materials.

### Is your ARC team activated?

ARC distribution checklist: ARC team identified and contacted (minimum 15-20 people for a target of 8-12 launch-day reviews). Manuscript files prepared in reader-friendly formats (EPUB and MOBI, or BookFunnel link). ARC email sent with: book files, publication date, requested review posting date, Amazon link for posting, and a reminder that honest reviews are expected and appreciated. ARC disclosure reminder included ("I received an advance copy in exchange for an honest review").

### Is your email launch ready?

Email preparation checklist: launch email written with a personal tone, clear book description, and direct Amazon buy link. Email scheduled for launch-day morning. Subject line tested — it should create curiosity or urgency ("It is finally here" outperforms "New book available"). Preview text optimized (the first line readers see in their inbox). Unsubscribe link and CAN-SPAM compliance verified.

### Is your social media content prepared?

Social media checklist: cover reveal post created and scheduled (if not already posted). Launch-day announcement post written for each platform. "Why I wrote this book" personal post drafted. Quote graphic or excerpt graphic created. Content calendar for launch week mapped out — at least one post per day across your primary platform.

## Phase 3: Final preparation (2 days before launch)

### Are all systems go?

Two-day checklist: ARC reminder sent — friendly message with the launch date, Amazon review link, and a thank-you for their time. Email launch blast final check — all links working, formatting correct, send time confirmed. Social media posts queued and scheduled. Amazon Ads campaign drafted and ready to activate (if running paid advertising). Paperback proof ordered or approved (if offering print edition).

### Have you tested everything?

Testing checklist: click every link in your email, social posts, and book description. Verify the preorder page displays correctly on desktop and mobile. Check Look Inside preview on Amazon — the first pages readers see should be compelling and error-free. Verify your author bio on Amazon Author Central is current and complete.

## Phase 4: Launch day

### Morning tasks

Launch morning checklist: verify book is live on Amazon (status changed from preorder to available). Check listing: cover rendering, description formatting, pricing, categories. Claim book on Author Central. Send email launch blast. Post primary launch announcement on social media.

### Midday tasks

Midday checklist: send ARC team reminder — "The book is live! Here is the direct link to post your review." Monitor first reviews appearing. Share any early reader reactions on social media. Respond to comments, messages, and engagement on all platforms.

### Evening tasks

Evening checklist: share launch-day milestones on social media (first reviews, first sales numbers if comfortable sharing). Thank everyone who helped — ARC readers, supporters, early buyers. Activate Amazon Ads campaign if planned. Take a screenshot of your first Amazon review for future marketing use.

## Phase 5: Launch week (days 2-7)

### Daily launch week tasks

Daily checklist: post at least one piece of content on your primary social platform — a new angle on the book each day (behind the scenes, a reader quote, an excerpt, a question for your audience). Monitor and respond to reviews. Check Amazon Ads performance (if running) — pause underperforming keywords, note high-performing ones. Engage with anyone who tags you, shares your book, or mentions it online.

### End-of-week assessment

Week-one review checklist: total sales and page reads recorded. Number of reviews counted. Amazon Best Seller Rank noted in each category. Ad spend versus revenue calculated (if running Amazon Ads). Social media engagement reviewed — which posts performed best? Email open rate and click-through rate reviewed. Notes written on what worked and what to improve for next launch.

## Phase 6: Post-launch (weeks 2-4)

### Sustaining momentum

Post-launch checklist: follow-up email sent to list (two weeks after launch) — "Have you read it yet? A short review helps other readers find it." Description revised if conversion rate is lower than expected — test a new hook or structure. Keywords adjusted based on first-week ad performance data. Additional Amazon categories requested through KDP support. Promotional pricing considered (Kindle Countdown Deal or $0.99 sale) for a week-three visibility boost.

### Planning forward

Forward planning checklist: launch retrospective completed — what went well, what needs improvement, and what will you do differently next time. Next book timeline established. Notes on new ARC contacts, reviewer relationships, and marketing channels discovered during launch. Revenue and expense tracking updated.

## Using this checklist for every launch

### How does the process improve over time?

Each launch teaches you something the last one did not. Your ARC team grows as readers who loved your book volunteer for the next one. Your email list grows as new readers subscribe through your back matter. Your social media audience grows as launch content attracts followers. Your understanding of Amazon Ads deepens with each campaign.

The checklist stays the same. Your execution gets sharper. By your third or fourth launch, the process feels routine — and routine is exactly what you want. A launch that feels routine is a launch where nothing is forgotten, nothing is rushed, and every lever is pulled at the right time.
