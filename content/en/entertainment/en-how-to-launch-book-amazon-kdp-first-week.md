# How to Launch a Book on Amazon KDP: The First-Week Playbook

There is a moment in every self-published author's career that separates the books that find readers from the books that disappear. It is not the moment you finish writing. It is not the moment you hit "Publish." It is the seven days that follow publication — the window when Amazon's algorithm is watching your book more closely than it ever will again, deciding whether to amplify your visibility or let you sink into the catalog of four million other titles.

Most indie authors waste this window. They publish, post once on social media, and wait. The algorithm registers minimal activity, assigns minimal visibility, and the book enters a death spiral of low sales → low visibility → lower sales → lower visibility. Within weeks, the book is functionally invisible without paid advertising.

A planned launch reverses this dynamic entirely. Concentrated effort in the first seven days creates a burst of sales and reviews that the algorithm interprets as a signal of quality and reader interest. It responds with organic visibility — "also bought" recommendations, category ranking boosts, "customers who viewed this" placements — that drives additional sales without additional effort. A strong first week creates a flywheel that spins for months.

Here is how to build that flywheel.

## 30 days before launch

### What should you have ready one month out?

Thirty days before your publication date, four things must be finalized: your manuscript (fully edited, formatted, and exported to EPUB and PDF), your cover (final version, sized for both ebook and paperback), your book description (written, HTML-formatted, and tested against competitor descriptions), and your metadata (seven keyword phrases and three browse categories researched and selected).

If any of these are not ready, push your launch date. Publishing before your product is polished wastes your first-week window on a version that is not ready for prime time. You cannot re-launch.

With these assets ready, set up your book as a preorder on KDP. Preorders allow you to accumulate early sales that count toward your launch-day ranking, and they give you a live Amazon page to link to in your pre-launch marketing. Set the preorder for 30 days out — long enough to build anticipation, short enough to maintain urgency.

### How do you start building launch momentum?

Day 30 is when your ARC team receives copies. Send the manuscript via BookFunnel or email, with a clear message: the publication date, the requested review posting date (launch day), and a reminder that honest reviews are expected. Include the book's cover, description, and a brief note about why this book matters to you — ARC readers who feel connected to the author are more likely to follow through.

Begin teasing the book on social media. Not a hard sell — a reveal. Share the cover with a story about the design process. Post a paragraph from the opening chapter. Share the inspiration behind the book. The goal is to create awareness and curiosity in the two to three weeks before launch, so that your launch-day announcement lands with an audience that already knows what the book is and is primed to care.

If you have an email list, send a pre-launch announcement: "My next book launches on [date]. Here is what it is about, why I wrote it, and how you can be part of the launch."

## Two weeks before launch

### What intensifies in the final stretch?

Two weeks out, the pace increases. Send a reminder to your ARC team — a friendly, specific message with the launch date and a direct link to where they will post their review. ARC readers are volunteers. They need reminders, not demands.

Submit your book to promotional newsletter services if you are running a launch deal. BookBub Featured Deals have long lead times (submit 6-8 weeks early for the best chance), but secondary services — Freebooksy, Robin Reads, Bargain Booksy, Book Barbarian — accept submissions with shorter notice. A promotional feature on launch day or during launch week can drive hundreds of additional sales.

Prepare your email launch blast. Write it now, schedule it for launch morning. The email should be personal, enthusiastic, and direct: what the book is, why it matters, and a clear call to action ("Buy it here" with a direct Amazon link). Do not overthink it — warmth and clarity outperform marketing polish.

Schedule your social media launch posts. Prepare at least three posts: a launch-day announcement, a "why I wrote this" personal story, and a call for reviews or shares. Platform-specific considerations: BookTok favors behind-the-scenes video and emotional storytelling; Instagram favors aesthetic cover reveals and quote graphics; Facebook favors longer personal narratives and direct community engagement.

## Launch day

### What is the hour-by-hour plan?

Launch morning: verify your book is live on Amazon. Check the listing — cover rendering, description formatting, Look Inside preview, pricing, and categories. Fix anything that needs fixing. Claim the book on Author Central, which gives you access to enhanced description editing and sales data.

Mid-morning: send your email blast. This is typically your highest-conversion channel — email subscribers are your most engaged audience, and a launch-day email with a direct buy link converts at 5-15%. Time it for mid-morning when people are checking email at work (counterintuitively, this works — people buy ebooks during work breaks).

Late morning: post your primary launch announcement on social media. Lead with emotion, not information. "This book has been living in my head for a year. Today it is yours" outperforms "My new book is available on Amazon!" The first creates emotional connection. The second creates noise.

Afternoon: remind your ARC team that the book is live and reviews can now be posted. Provide a direct link to the review page. Many ARC readers wait for a reminder — this is normal, not rude.

Evening: share early reviews on social media. If readers or ARC team members have messaged you with feedback, quote their reactions (with permission). Real reader responses are the most compelling social proof you can post.

Throughout the day: respond to every message, comment, and engagement. Launch day is when your personal attention matters most. Readers who feel seen and appreciated become long-term fans.

### What if you have no email list and no ARC team?

You launch anyway — but you launch smarter. Without an existing audience, your first-week strategy shifts toward paid and earned visibility.

Paid: start an Amazon Ads campaign on launch day with a modest budget ($10-20/day). Target keywords in your genre and competitor book ASINs. Amazon Ads put your book in front of readers who are already shopping for books like yours. Monitor performance daily and adjust bids based on which keywords convert.

Earned: reach out directly to book bloggers, BookTok creators, and Bookstagram accounts that review books in your genre. Send a personalized message (not a copy-paste template) explaining your book and offering a free copy for review consideration. Response rates are low (5-10%), but a single enthusiastic review from a creator with 5,000 followers can drive dozens of sales.

Consider a $0.99 launch price for the first three days. Lower price reduces the barrier to impulse purchase and drives higher volume — volume that the algorithm reads as velocity. After the initial burst, raise to your target price.

## Days 2-7: sustaining momentum

### How do you keep the flywheel spinning after day one?

The worst thing you can do in your second week is nothing. The algorithm is still watching. Each additional sale and review in days two through seven reinforces the signal that your book deserves visibility.

Day 2-3: post follow-up social content. Share a different angle on the book — a chapter preview, a reader reaction, a behind-the-scenes story about the writing process. Fresh content keeps your launch visible in social feeds that have already scrolled past your day-one announcement.

Day 3-4: if reviews are appearing, share screenshots on social media. New reviews are newsworthy to your audience and serve as social proof for undecided buyers.

Day 5-7: evaluate your Amazon Ads performance. Pause underperforming keywords. Increase budget on keywords that are converting. By day five, you have enough data to optimize intelligently.

Throughout the week: continue engaging with readers. Answer questions. Thank reviewers. Share milestones ("50 copies sold this week!" is genuine and encouraging, not bragging). Your visibility and enthusiasm during launch week set the tone for your book's entire commercial life.

## Post-launch: weeks 2-4

### What keeps the momentum going after launch week?

The first month after launch is about converting initial velocity into sustained baseline sales. Three strategies drive this conversion.

First, continue building reviews. Your back matter should include a review request. Send a follow-up email to your list two weeks after launch: "If you have read [book title], a short review on Amazon helps other readers find it." Every additional review improves your conversion rate — the percentage of page visitors who buy.

Second, optimize your listing based on first-week data. Which keywords are driving impressions and clicks? Which are not? Adjust your seven keyword phrases based on actual performance. Rewrite your description if the conversion rate (clicks to sales) is lower than expected — experiment with a new hook, a different structure, or stronger calls to action.

Third, plan your next book. The single most powerful marketing tool for any self-published book is the next book in the series or catalog. Each new release drives discovery of your previous titles. Authors who maintain a consistent publishing cadence — every 60-90 days — build compounding momentum that no amount of advertising can replicate for a standalone title.
