## The electromagnetic ocean we swim in

Right now, as you read these words, your body is being penetrated by thousands of invisible signals. Wi-Fi routers, cell towers, Bluetooth devices, GPS satellites. The air around you is thick with electromagnetic radiation, all operating at frequencies your senses cannot detect.

We have collectively decided this is fine. And for the most part, it is. But science fiction has always been at its most powerful when it takes a current reality and asks: what if we are wrong?

## The frontier of neurotechnology

The idea of signals affecting the brain is not pure fantasy. Transcranial magnetic stimulation already uses electromagnetic pulses to treat depression. Brain-computer interfaces are advancing rapidly. The gap between therapeutic brain stimulation and broadcasting a signal that alters cognition at scale is enormous, but it is measured in engineering challenges, not physical impossibility.

This is precisely the territory that *Dead Frequency* explores. Not the impossible, but the improbable-but-plausible.

## When fiction becomes a warning

The best science fiction does not predict the future. It illuminates the present. In the same tradition, *Dead Frequency* by Adrian Phoenix Vale uses the near-future setting not as escapism, but as a lens. The questions it raises about invisible infrastructure, about trust in systems we do not understand, about the line between connectivity and vulnerability, are urgently real.
