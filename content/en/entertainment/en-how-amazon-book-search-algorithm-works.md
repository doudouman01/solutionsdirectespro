# How Amazon's Book Search Algorithm Works: What Every Author Needs to Know

Every time a reader types a query into Amazon's search bar, an algorithm decides which books appear, in which order, and on which page. Your book is either in those results or it is not. And the difference between appearing on page one (where 70-80% of clicks happen) and appearing on page five (where almost nobody scrolls) is not random. It is determined by a set of ranking signals that the algorithm weighs, combines, and updates in real time.

Amazon does not publish the exact formula behind its search algorithm (known internally as A9, though the name is used less frequently in recent years). But years of testing, observation, and data analysis by the indie author community — combined with Amazon's own guidance and patent filings — have revealed the core ranking factors with reasonable confidence.

Understanding these factors does not guarantee page-one placement. But not understanding them guarantees you are competing blind against authors who do.

## The ranking signals

### How does keyword relevance affect your ranking?

Relevance is the foundation. Amazon's algorithm first filters the entire catalog to find books that match the search query. This matching happens across multiple fields: your book title, subtitle, series name, author name, book description, and — critically — your seven keyword metadata fields.

A book whose metadata closely matches the search query is eligible to rank. A book with no relevant metadata match is invisible to that search, regardless of how popular or well-reviewed it is. This is why keyword selection is so fundamental: your keywords determine which searches your book is eligible to appear in.

The algorithm evaluates not just exact matches but semantic relevance — phrases that are closely related to the query. Searching "enemies to lovers romance" may also surface books with keywords like "hate to love romance" or "rivals to lovers." But exact and near-exact matches rank higher than loosely related ones. The closer your keyword phrases are to what readers actually type, the stronger your relevance signal.

### How do sales velocity and history affect ranking?

After relevance, sales are the strongest ranking signal. Books that are selling — recently and consistently — rank higher than books that are not. Amazon's algorithm interprets sales as a quality and demand signal: if readers are buying this book, it is probably good and probably relevant to the search.

Sales velocity (how fast sales are accumulating) matters more than total sales history. A book that sold 50 copies yesterday outranks a book that sold 5,000 copies last year for most search queries. This is why launch-week velocity is so critical — a burst of sales in a short period creates a strong velocity signal that boosts ranking for weeks afterward.

Kindle Unlimited page reads contribute to this signal alongside direct sales. For KU-enrolled books, page reads are essentially equivalent to sales in the algorithm's ranking calculations. This is one reason KU enrollment is so advantageous in genres with high KU readership — page reads boost your ranking signal without requiring readers to purchase the book outright.

### How do reviews influence search ranking?

Reviews affect ranking both directly and indirectly. Directly, books with more reviews and higher average ratings tend to rank higher for competitive search queries, all else being equal. The algorithm treats review count as a popularity signal and rating as a quality signal.

Indirectly, reviews affect your conversion rate — the percentage of page visitors who buy. A book with 50 positive reviews converts significantly more browsers into buyers than an identical book with zero reviews. Higher conversion means more sales, which means a stronger velocity signal, which means higher ranking. Reviews create a compound effect that multiplies through the algorithm.

The quality of reviews matters more than quantity beyond a baseline threshold. A book with 20 detailed, specific reviews from verified purchasers signals authenticity more strongly than a book with 100 one-line generic reviews. Amazon's algorithm has become increasingly sophisticated at evaluating review quality and detecting inauthentic patterns.

### What role does click-through rate play?

When your book appears in search results, the algorithm tracks how often it gets clicked relative to how often it is shown (impressions). A book that gets clicked 10% of the time ranks higher than a book clicked 2% of the time, because the higher click rate signals that the book's presentation (cover, title, price) is more appealing to readers searching this query.

Your click-through rate is determined almost entirely by your cover and your title. These are the only elements visible in search results before a reader clicks. A professional, genre-appropriate cover that stands out at thumbnail size drives a higher click rate, which drives higher ranking, which drives more impressions — a self-reinforcing loop that begins with cover quality.

### How does pricing affect ranking?

Price does not directly affect ranking in most cases. A $4.99 book does not inherently rank higher or lower than a $2.99 book for the same keyword. However, price affects conversion rate (lower prices generally convert higher), and conversion rate affects sales velocity, which affects ranking.

During promotional pricing — $0.99 sales, free promotions, Kindle Countdown Deals — the increased conversion rate and sales volume create a temporary velocity boost that can significantly improve ranking. This is why promotional pricing is a standard launch and re-launch tactic: the temporary price reduction drives a sales burst that the algorithm rewards with sustained visibility at regular pricing.

## Category ranking

### How does category ranking differ from search ranking?

Search ranking determines where your book appears when a reader searches a specific keyword. Category ranking determines where your book appears within Amazon's browse categories — the hierarchical genre listings that readers navigate when browsing rather than searching.

Category ranking is based primarily on sales velocity relative to other books in the same category. The number-one book in "Romance > Contemporary > Small Town" is the book in that category with the highest recent sales velocity. Category rankings update hourly, which means a concentrated burst of sales can temporarily push your book to the top of a category — giving it the "Best Seller" or "Hot New Release" tag that dramatically increases visibility and click-through rate.

This is another reason why category selection matters. A book ranked number 47 in "Romance" is invisible. The same book ranked number 3 in "Romance > Contemporary > Small Town > Western" has a best-seller tag and appears prominently in the category's featured listings.

### How do keywords trigger category placement?

Amazon uses your keyword metadata to supplement your manually selected categories. Certain keyword phrases trigger automatic placement in browse categories that are not available through the standard category dropdown during KDP upload.

These trigger phrases change periodically, but examples include: "African American romance" triggering placement in the African American Romance category, "clean romance" triggering placement in Clean & Wholesome Romance, or "time travel" triggering placement in Time Travel Science Fiction. Research current trigger phrases for your genre — indie author communities actively track and share these as Amazon updates them.

You can also request category additions through KDP support after publication, specifying up to ten total categories. Combining manual selection, keyword triggers, and support requests gives you maximum category coverage — and therefore maximum browse visibility.

## The recommendation algorithm

### How does "customers also bought" work?

Amazon's recommendation engine tracks purchasing patterns across its entire customer base. When a reader buys your book, the algorithm notes what other books that reader has purchased. When enough readers buy both your book and another specific book, the algorithm creates an "also bought" connection between them.

These connections are enormously valuable because they represent free, targeted visibility. Your book appears on the product pages of related books, in front of readers who have already demonstrated interest in similar content. The "also bought" section is one of the highest-converting placements on Amazon — readers who click there are pre-qualified by their purchasing history.

Your keywords influence which books you get connected to through "also bought." Books with similar keyword profiles are more likely to share buyers, which builds "also bought" connections faster. This is why targeting the same keywords as your closest competitors (books that appeal to the same readers) is strategically valuable — it positions your book to appear alongside theirs in recommendations.

## Optimizing for the algorithm

### What is the most effective overall strategy?

The algorithm rewards books that readers want. Every optimization tactic — keywords, categories, pricing, reviews, launch velocity — is ultimately about connecting the right book with the right readers more efficiently. The fundamental requirements are: a book worth reading (quality content), a listing worth clicking (professional cover and compelling description), and metadata worth matching (relevant, specific keywords in the right categories).

No amount of keyword optimization will save a book with a bad cover. No amount of category strategy will overcome a misleading description. The algorithm amplifies quality — it does not create it. Start with a great product, then optimize its discoverability.

### How often does the algorithm change?

Amazon continuously adjusts its algorithm, typically in small increments rather than dramatic overhauls. Major shifts in ranking factors are rare, but the relative weight of different signals evolves. The community occasionally observes changes: a period where review count seems to matter more, a period where sales velocity seems to dominate, a period where click-through rate appears more influential.

The safest long-term strategy is to optimize for all signals simultaneously rather than trying to game any single one. A book with strong keywords, a professional cover, a converting description, consistent reviews, and steady sales velocity is resilient to algorithmic changes — because it satisfies every signal the algorithm has ever used.
