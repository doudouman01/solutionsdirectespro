# 15 Copy-Paste ChatGPT Prompts for Real Estate Agents That Actually Close Deals

Most real estate agents use ChatGPT wrong. They type something vague like "write a listing description" and get back generic copy that could describe any property in any city. Then they conclude AI doesn't work for real estate.

The problem isn't the tool. It's the prompt.

A well-structured prompt gives ChatGPT enough context to produce copy you can actually use — polished, specific, and tailored to your market, your brand, and your clients. The difference between a weak prompt and a strong one is the difference between spending 20 minutes editing AI output and spending 2 minutes fine-tuning it.

Here are 15 prompts built specifically for real estate agents, covering every stage of the client lifecycle — from lead capture to closing.

## Listing & Property Marketing Prompts

### Prompt 1: MLS Listing Description

```
You are a senior real estate copywriter specializing in [YOUR MARKET] properties.

Write a 150-word MLS listing description for a [BEDROOMS]-bedroom, [BATHROOMS]-bathroom [HOME STYLE] in [NEIGHBORHOOD/CITY].

Key features:
- [FEATURE 1]
- [FEATURE 2]
- [FEATURE 3]
- [FEATURE 4]

Target buyer: [PROFILE — e.g., move-up family, young professional, downsizer, investor]

Tone: [warm / luxury / practical / energetic]

Requirements:
- Lead with the strongest emotional hook, not square footage
- Include the neighborhood name naturally in the first two sentences
- End with a soft call to action
- Avoid clichés: no "stunning," "turnkey," or "won't last long"
- After the description, flag any phrases that could raise Fair Housing concerns
```

### Prompt 2: Social Media Carousel — Just Listed

```
Create a 5-slide Instagram carousel for a "Just Listed" post.

Property: [ADDRESS], [BEDS/BATHS], [KEY FEATURES], listed at [PRICE]

Slide 1: Hook headline (stop the scroll — make it about the lifestyle, not the specs)
Slide 2: 3 standout features with one-line descriptions
Slide 3: Neighborhood highlights — walkability, schools, dining, parks
Slide 4: "Who this home is perfect for" — describe the ideal buyer persona
Slide 5: CTA with my contact info: [YOUR NAME, PHONE, EMAIL]

Tone: conversational but professional. Write for Instagram, not MLS.
Include 15 relevant hashtags mixing hyper-local (#[NEIGHBORHOOD]Homes) and broad (#JustListed).
```

### Prompt 3: Property Video Script (60 seconds)

```
Write a 60-second video walkthrough script for [PROPERTY ADDRESS].

Format: casual, first-person narration as if I'm walking a friend through the home.

Key selling points to highlight:
- [FEATURE 1]
- [FEATURE 2]
- [FEATURE 3]

Open with a hook that makes viewers want to see more (not "Welcome to 123 Main Street").
Close with a CTA to schedule a private showing.

Keep it under 180 words — I'll be speaking while walking, so shorter sentences work better.
```

## Lead Generation & Follow-Up Prompts

### Prompt 4: Open House Follow-Up Email

```
Write a follow-up email to [FIRST NAME], who attended my open house yesterday at [PROPERTY ADDRESS].

During the showing, they mentioned interest in [SPECIFIC FEATURE OR CONCERN — e.g., the backyard size, school district, kitchen renovation potential].

Goals:
- Reference something specific from our conversation (not generic)
- Provide one piece of additional value (a comparable listing, neighborhood data, or market insight)
- Include a soft CTA to schedule a private showing or coffee meeting
- Keep it under 150 words
- Tone: personal and warm, not salesy

Sign off as [YOUR NAME], [BROKERAGE]
```

### Prompt 5: Cold Lead Re-Engagement Sequence (3 emails)

```
Write a 3-email re-engagement sequence for a buyer lead who went cold 60+ days ago.

Lead profile: [WHAT YOU KNOW — e.g., looking for 3BR in [AREA], budget $500K, first-time buyer]

Each email must:
- Be under 100 words
- Provide genuine value (not just "checking in")
- Have a different angle

Email 1: Share a relevant market insight or new listing they'd find interesting
Email 2: Offer a piece of useful content (neighborhood guide, market report, buying timeline)
Email 3: "Closing the loop" — honest, non-pushy acknowledgment that priorities change, leaving the door open

Never use: "just checking in," "circling back," "touching base"
```

### Prompt 6: Buyer Lead Qualification Questions

```
Create 12 strategic questions for an initial buyer consultation, organized in this order:

1-3: Rapport-building (lifestyle, what brought them to the area)
4-6: Motivation and timeline (why now, urgency, decision-making process)
7-9: Financial readiness (pre-approval status, budget comfort, hidden costs awareness)
10-12: Deal-breakers and must-haves (non-negotiables vs. nice-to-haves)

For each question, add a one-line note explaining what the answer reveals about lead quality.

Format as a table: Question | What the answer tells you
```

## Content & Authority-Building Prompts

### Prompt 7: Monthly Market Update Newsletter

```
Write a 300-word monthly market update for [CITY/NEIGHBORHOOD] — [MONTH YEAR].

Use this data:
- Median sale price: [AMOUNT] ([UP/DOWN X%] from last month)
- Active listings: [NUMBER]
- Average days on market: [NUMBER]
- List-to-sale price ratio: [PERCENTAGE]

Structure:
1. One-sentence headline takeaway (what this data means for buyers AND sellers)
2. Three key data points with plain-language interpretation
3. One actionable recommendation for each: buyers and sellers
4. Closing CTA to contact me for a personalized analysis

Tone: knowledgeable local expert, not corporate. Write like you're explaining this to a smart friend over coffee.
```

### Prompt 8: Neighborhood Guide (Pillar Content)

```
Create a comprehensive neighborhood guide for [NEIGHBORHOOD], [CITY] targeting [BUYER TYPE].

Include:
- Overview: vibe, character, what makes it unique (200 words)
- Schools: top-rated options with grades served
- Dining: 5 local favorites (not chains) with one-line descriptions
- Outdoor: parks, trails, recreation
- Commute: drive times and transit options to [MAJOR EMPLOYMENT CENTER]
- Market snapshot: typical price range, home styles, lot sizes
- Insider tip: something only a local would know

Length: 800-1,000 words
Tone: enthusiastic local expert who genuinely knows and loves this area

After the guide, suggest 5 social media post ideas derived from this content.
```

### Prompt 9: Google Business Profile Review Request

```
Write a brief text message asking a past client for a Google review.

Context: I helped [CLIENT NAME] [buy/sell] their [PROPERTY TYPE] in [NEIGHBORHOOD] [TIMEFRAME — e.g., "three months ago"].

Requirements:
- Keep it under 50 words
- Reference our specific transaction (not generic)
- Express genuine gratitude
- Make it easy — include the placeholder [REVIEW LINK]
- Don't be pushy — one ask, no guilt

Write 3 versions with slightly different tones: professional, warm, casual.
```

## Negotiation & Objection Handling Prompts

### Prompt 10: "Your Commission Is Too High" Response

```
Give me 3 responses to the seller objection: "Your commission is too high. Another agent offered [LOWER RATE]."

For each response:
1. Acknowledge the concern without being defensive
2. Demonstrate specific value that justifies the rate
3. Redirect to results and ROI
4. End with a question that re-engages the conversation

Tone: confident, not arrogant. Never speak negatively about the other agent.
Keep each response under 100 words — this is a conversation, not a monologue.
```

### Prompt 11: Buyer Objection — "We Want to Wait for Prices to Drop"

```
Give me 3 responses to: "We're going to wait — prices have to come down eventually."

Each response should:
1. Validate their concern (don't dismiss it)
2. Present one data point or market reality that challenges the assumption
3. Frame the cost of waiting (opportunity cost, rate changes, inventory shifts)
4. Offer a low-pressure next step

Avoid: scare tactics, pressure language, or anything that sounds like "buy now or regret it forever."
```

## Client Communication Prompts

### Prompt 12: Pre-Listing Consultation Prep

```
Prepare a pre-listing consultation outline for a homeowner selling a [PROPERTY TYPE] in [NEIGHBORHOOD].

Include:
1. Opening: 3 rapport-building questions about their home and experience living there
2. Market context: talking points for current [NEIGHBORHOOD] market conditions
3. Pricing strategy: framework for discussing pricing (not specific numbers)
4. Marketing plan: 7 things I'll do that other agents might not
5. Timeline: realistic week-by-week breakdown from listing to closing
6. The "why me" close: 3 differentiators that position me above competitors

Format as a one-page outline I can reference during the meeting — bullet points, not paragraphs.
```

### Prompt 13: Closing Day Thank You Message

```
Write a heartfelt closing day message to [CLIENT NAME(S)] who just [bought/sold] their [PROPERTY TYPE] in [NEIGHBORHOOD].

Reference:
- Something specific about their journey (e.g., "I know finding a home with [FEATURE] was important to you")
- One memorable moment from our working together
- A warm wish for their future in the new home

Include a subtle referral ask — something natural like "If anyone you know is thinking about a move, I'd love to help them the way I got to help you."

Keep it under 100 words. Genuine, not templated.
```

### Prompt 14: Seller Weekly Update Email

```
Write a weekly seller update email template I can customize each week.

Structure:
- Opening: one sentence acknowledging the week
- Showing activity: [NUMBER] showings this week, [FEEDBACK SUMMARY]
- Online performance: [VIEWS], [SAVES], [INQUIRIES] on major platforms
- Market context: one relevant data point (new comparable sale, inventory change)
- Next steps: what I'm doing this coming week to keep momentum
- Closing: reassurance + availability for questions

Tone: transparent and proactive. Sellers need to feel informed, not abandoned.
Under 200 words.
```

### Prompt 15: "We're Thinking About Selling" Nurture Sequence

```
Create a 4-email nurture sequence for a homeowner who mentioned they're "thinking about selling" but isn't ready yet. Space the emails 2 weeks apart.

Email 1: "What your home might be worth right now" — offer a free, no-obligation market analysis
Email 2: "3 things smart sellers do 90 days before listing" — provide actionable prep advice
Email 3: Share a recent success story from the same neighborhood (anonymized)
Email 4: "No pressure, just an update" — share one market insight and leave the door open

Each email: under 120 words, one clear CTA, never pushy.
Tone: patient expert, not hungry salesperson.
```

## How to Get Even Better Results from AI

These prompts work because they follow a structure that most agents skip: **Role → Context → Format → Anti-pattern** (what to avoid). The more specific you are about who the AI should be, what it's writing about, how to format the output, and what mistakes to avoid, the better the result.

But prompts are just the starting point. The real leverage comes from building AI into your daily workflow as a system — not a novelty you use when you remember.
