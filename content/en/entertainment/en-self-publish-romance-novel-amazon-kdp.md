# How to Self-Publish a Romance Novel on Amazon KDP: The Complete Step-by-Step Guide

Amazon KDP controls approximately 80% of the ebook market in the United States. For romance authors, that dominance is even more pronounced — romance is KDP's single most profitable fiction category, and Kindle Unlimited (exclusive to KDP Select) is where the majority of romance reading happens. If you are publishing a romance novel in 2026, Amazon is not optional. It is the platform.

The good news is that publishing on KDP is technically simple. Upload a manuscript, add a cover, write a description, set a price, click publish. The bad news is that simplicity creates a false sense of completion. A book that is merely published on KDP is invisible. A book that is strategically published on KDP — with optimized keywords, a genre-appropriate cover, a converting blurb, correct category placement, and smart pricing — has a fighting chance at reaching readers.

This guide walks through every decision point between finished manuscript and first sale.

## Preparing your manuscript

### What format should your manuscript be in?

KDP accepts EPUB, DOCX, and KPF (Kindle Create format) files. For romance novels, EPUB is the recommended format because it gives you the most control over formatting and produces the cleanest result on all Kindle devices.

The most efficient workflow for indie romance authors is: write in your preferred word processor, format in a tool like Atticus, Vellum (Mac only), or Kindle Create, and export to EPUB. These tools handle chapter breaks, table of contents, front matter, and back matter formatting automatically — eliminating the formatting errors that plague DOCX uploads.

If you upload a DOCX file, expect to spend significant time fixing formatting issues: inconsistent paragraph spacing, missing or broken table of contents, image positioning problems, and font rendering differences across devices. For a professional result with minimal frustration, invest in a formatting tool.

### What should your front matter and back matter contain?

Front matter for romance should be minimal: title page, copyright page, and optionally a dedication or content warning. Romance readers want to get to the story quickly — a three-page foreword, acknowledgments, and author bio before chapter one is a reader-hostile choice.

Back matter is where the real business happens. After "The End," your book should contain: a note from the author (brief, personal, inviting), a link to your next book or series, a link to your email list signup, a request to leave a review, and links to your social media. Back matter is the highest-converting sales real estate you own — a reader who just finished your book and loved it is the most receptive buyer in all of publishing. Do not waste that moment with a generic "Thanks for reading."

## Creating your cover

### How important is the cover for romance sales?

The cover is the single most important marketing asset for a romance novel. It is more important than the title, the blurb, the keywords, and the reviews — because it is the first thing a reader sees, and romance readers make split-second judgments about genre, subgenre, heat level, and quality based entirely on the cover.

Romance covers communicate through a highly developed visual language. Illustrated covers signal contemporary and romantasy. Photography covers signal romantic suspense and erotica. Typography-dominant covers signal sweet or literary romance. Color palettes communicate mood: pastels for light contemporary, dark backgrounds for dark romance, warm tones for historical.

A cover that violates genre conventions — no matter how beautiful it is — will fail because romance readers will scroll past it. They will not even stop long enough to read the title. Your cover must look like it belongs on the same shelf as the current bestsellers in your specific subgenre. This is not a creative choice — it is a market communication requirement.

### Should you design your own cover or hire a professional?

Hire a professional. Without exception. A professional romance cover designer (not a general graphic designer — a romance-specific cover designer) costs $150-500 and understands the visual language of the genre. They know which stock images are overused, which fonts signal which subgenres, which trends are current, and which compositions convert browsers into buyers.

Self-designed covers are the number one reason romance novels fail to gain traction. Even a well-written book with a great blurb will die with an amateur cover, because readers will never click on it to discover the blurb or the writing.

## Optimizing your KDP listing

### How do you write a blurb that converts?

A romance blurb is not a plot summary — it is a sales pitch. Its job is to make a reader who stopped scrolling (because the cover caught their eye) feel compelled to click "Buy Now." You have approximately 150-200 words before the "Read more" fold. Every word must earn its place.

The proven structure for romance blurbs: open with a hook that establishes the protagonist's emotional situation and voice. Introduce the love interest in a way that immediately signals the central dynamic (the tension, the attraction, the impossibility). Escalate the stakes — what is at risk emotionally? End with a question or statement that creates urgency ("But when the truth comes out, will their love survive — or was it built on lies from the start?").

Avoid plot details. Avoid character backstory. Avoid anything that does not directly serve the emotional hook. The reader does not need to understand the plot to buy the book — they need to feel the promise of an emotional experience they want to have.

### How do Amazon keywords work for romance?

KDP allows you to enter seven keyword phrases (each up to 50 characters). These keywords determine which searches your book appears in and which categories Amazon assigns you to. For romance, keyword strategy is critical because the genre has hundreds of specific niches that readers search for by name.

Effective romance keywords combine trope, subgenre, and reader language: "enemies to lovers dark romance," "small town second chance," "billionaire arranged marriage," "slow burn fantasy romance." Each keyword phrase should target a different search query that your ideal reader might use.

Do not waste keyword slots on generic terms like "romance novel" or "love story" — the competition for these terms is enormous, and readers searching them are not targeted enough to convert well. Specificity wins in romance keyword strategy.

### What categories should you choose?

Amazon allows you to select up to three browse categories during the KDP upload process. Choose the most specific categories available that accurately describe your book. "Romance > Contemporary > New Adult" is better than "Romance > Contemporary" because the competition pool is smaller and your book is more likely to rank.

After publication, you can request up to ten total categories by contacting KDP support. This is standard practice among successful indie romance authors. More categories mean more visibility with zero downside.

## Pricing and enrollment decisions

### Should you enroll in KDP Select?

KDP Select enrolls your book in Kindle Unlimited (KU) and makes it exclusive to Amazon for 90-day terms. For romance authors, the decision is straightforward in most cases: yes, especially for new authors.

Romance dominates KU readership. The majority of romance readers subscribe to KU and consume most of their books through it. KU page reads typically generate 2-3x the revenue of direct sales for romance titles. And KU enrollment gives you access to promotional tools (Kindle Countdown Deals, Free Book Promotions) that non-enrolled titles cannot use.

The tradeoff is exclusivity — you cannot sell on any other platform while enrolled. For authors who have established audiences on Kobo, Apple Books, or Google Play, wide distribution may generate more total revenue. But for new authors building an audience, KU's discoverability advantages in the romance genre are difficult to replicate elsewhere.

### How should you price your romance novel?

For a full-length romance novel (50,000-80,000 words), the standard indie pricing is $3.99-4.99 for Kindle, with $4.99 being the most common for established authors and $3.99 for new authors building readership.

Below $2.99, you earn a 35% royalty instead of 70%. Above $9.99, the same 35% rate applies. The $2.99-9.99 range is the 70% royalty sweet spot. For series, a common strategy is pricing book one at $0.99 or free to maximize reader acquisition, then pricing subsequent books at $4.99 to maximize revenue from readers who are already invested.

Paperback pricing for romance typically ranges from $12.99-16.99 depending on page count. Most romance revenue comes from Kindle and KU, but paperback availability adds credibility and captures the segment of readers who prefer physical books.

## Launching your romance novel

### What is the ideal launch timeline?

A successful romance launch begins 30 days before publication. Here is the sequence that maximizes first-week velocity:

Thirty days out: finalize cover, write blurb, set up preorder on KDP. Share cover reveal on social media and in reader groups. Begin distributing ARCs (Advance Review Copies) to your ARC team.

Two weeks out: send reminder to ARC readers with a requested review date (launch day). Prepare email blast for your mailing list. Submit to promotional newsletter services (BookBub, Freebooksy, Robin Reads) if running a deal.

Launch day: send your email blast. Remind ARC readers to post reviews on Amazon. Post across all social platforms. If running a $0.99 launch or Kindle Countdown Deal, activate it.

First week: monitor reviews and ranking daily. Engage with readers who reach out. Push any remaining promotional levers. Begin planning book two if this is a series.

### Why are first-week sales so important?

Amazon's algorithm uses initial sales velocity to determine how much organic visibility your book receives. A book that sells 100 copies in its first week will receive significantly more "also bought" recommendations, "customers who viewed this" placements, and category ranking boosts than a book that sells the same 100 copies spread over three months.

This is why concentrated launch efforts matter so much. Every sale and page read in the first seven days has an outsized impact on your book's long-term discoverability. A strong launch creates a virtuous cycle: sales drive visibility, visibility drives more sales, and the momentum compounds.
